Maxim is designed and built by **Denny Schaedig**, a neuroscientist and AI engineer.
The framework's bio-inspired architecture isn't a metaphor bolted on after the fact —
it comes directly from his training in how brains actually learn, form memories, and
consolidate experience.

## Background

Denny is a Colorado native who earned a **B.S. in Neuroscience** from the University of
Colorado, Boulder (2015), studying how the brain naturally learns and remembers, and
later a **master's in AI and machine learning** through Colorado State University Global
(2022). His work has sat at the intersection of the two ever since: since 2018 he has
applied computer vision and machine learning to brain-imaging research, including
training a model (KleinNet) to characterize brain function during cognitive tasks.

Today he works as a **data and AI engineer at the Laboratory for Child Brain
Development at Washington University in St. Louis**, where he preprocesses and analyzes
neuroimaging data (fMRI, fNIRS) using high-performance computing, statistics, and AI,
and has built open-source neuroimaging tooling and cloud compute infrastructure. Earlier
research roles span the Intermountain NeuroImaging Consortium and the Čeko, D'Mello, and
Banich labs.

## Why this shapes Maxim

The memory systems at the center of Maxim — the [hippocampus](/systems/hippocampus/) for
episodic memory, the [entorhinal cortex](/systems/entorhinal-cortex/),
[nucleus accumbens](/systems/nucleus-accumbens/) for reward learning, and the rest — map
to the same brain structures Denny studies professionally. Maxim is an attempt to take
what neuroscience knows about how experience becomes durable, transferable knowledge and
build it into an agent that learns across sessions without fine-tuning. The project is
run in a deliberately falsification-oriented way, with an open
[experiment log](/research/experiments/) that records what holds up and what doesn't —
including a robot that [learned to orient toward sound](/guides/reachy-mini/) in about ten
trials with no LLM in the action path.

## Other work

- **[HR_func_](https://www.dennyschaedig.com/projects)** — a tool for modeling hemodynamic response variability in fNIRS data (published in *Neurophotonics*)
- **synchronAI** — a multimodal model fusing video, audio, and fNIRS to detect interpersonal synchrony
- **AvAI™ / Rocky Mountain Digerati** — AI for avalanche-risk prediction from snowpack data, aimed at backcountry safety

His research on neural synchrony in parent–child dyads and on fNIRS modeling is listed
among his [publications](https://www.dennyschaedig.com/papers).
